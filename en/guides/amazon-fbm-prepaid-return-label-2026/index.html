Amazon FBM Prepaid Return Label Rule from 18 December 2026: 7-Day Deadline and Return vs Returnless Refund Calculator

From 18 Dec 2026 Amazon US sellers who fulfil their own orders must upload a prepaid return label within 7 days or Amazon may auto-refund the buyer. What changes, and a break-even calculator for return vs returnless refund.

SellerFee 26 October 2026 3 min read

From 18 December 2026, Amazon will require a prepaid return label on almost every US return of a seller-fulfilled (FBM) order. Sellers who give buyers only a return address, and let the buyer pay postage, will no longer meet the rule. This guide explains the change and includes a calculator to decide, return by return, whether to pay for the label or refund without getting the item back.

Amazon announced the change in an email to sellers, reported by PPC Land and Seller Essentials. We could not find a public Amazon page with the new rule: Seller Central help pages require a login, and PPC Land reports that the help page it reviewed on 19 October 2026 did not mention it yet. Check the notice in your Seller Central account.

What changes on 18 December 2026

  • All US returns for seller-fulfilled orders need a prepaid return shipping label. Address-only labels, where the customer arranges and pays for the return, no longer count.
  • Heavy and bulky items are excluded for now. Amazon calls this temporary and has not defined the category or given an end date.
  • Within 7 calendar days of each return request you must either upload a prepaid label against the request in Manage Seller-Fulfilled Returns, or issue a full refund without requiring the item back.
  • If you do neither, Amazon may issue a returnless refund automatically and deduct it from your selling account. The buyer keeps the item.
  • Labels sent through Buyer-Seller Messaging or any other channel outside the Manage Returns workflow do not count.

Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA) orders are not affected. Amazon already enrolls US sellers in prepaid returns through Buy Shipping; the new rule removes the address-only option that remained. Who finally pays for a label can still depend on the return reason, so check how Amazon treats seller-fault and buyer-remorse returns for your account.

Return or returnless refund? Break-even calculator

When a return comes in, you refund the buyer either way. The question is whether getting the item back is worth the label, the handling and the cost of selling it again.

Both options refund the buyer in full, so the refund itself is left out. The example values are illustrative: enter your own label cost and fees.

Worked examples

With a $8 label, $2 of handling, 15% selling fees, $5 to ship it again and an 80% chance it can be resold, a $25 item is worth getting back: the expected gain is $3.00. The break-even resale price is $20.59. Below that, a returnless refund costs you less. The same item at $12 would lose $5.84 on average if you paid for the return, so refund it without a return.

The formula: expected gain = chance of resale × (resale price × (1 − selling fees) − shipping to the next buyer) − label cost − handling.

Do not let the deadline decide for you

A returnless refund you choose for a cheap item is a sensible business decision. One that Amazon issues because nobody uploaded the label in 7 days can cost you an expensive item as well as the sale. Check open return requests every day, give the queue an owner and a backup, and set a price limit under which your team refunds without a return.

Checklist before 18 December

  • Find the products and procedures that still use address-only labels or send labels by message.
  • Make sure every label is uploaded in Manage Seller-Fulfilled Returns.
  • Work out your break-even resale price per product with the calculator above.
  • Check how Amazon classifies any products you think are heavy and bulky.
